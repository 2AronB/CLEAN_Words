## Review caveats and ambiguity notes

1. ORZO can be mistaken for rice. NIMBOSTRATUS is relatively specialized.
2. GOLD/SILVER are also colors; NEBULA/GALAXY are less familiar astronomy terms.
3. Compound-word board: FIRE and BOOK accept many other suffixes. FIREWORM and
   FIREMARK may be known specialist words, and BOOKPLACE can occur informally.
   Check complete partitions rather than assuming each tile has only one link.
4. Ballet/poetry vocabulary may be harder than everyday categories; accents in
   PLIE/JETE/SAUTE are intentionally omitted from uppercase tiles.
5. KAYAK is also a watercraft and CIVIC an adjective; only the spelled-word
   property is relevant. COBALT can also mean a metal.
6. Replaced a draft of four compound-prefix groups after finding an alternate
   full partition (MOONLIGHT/SUNRISE versus MOONRISE/SUNLIGHT). The final board
   mixes a SNOW compound group, bicycle parts, geometric solids and NESS words.
   SADDLE has other senses; the bicycle sense is intended.
7. FLOCK/HERD/SWARM/POD cover broad animal groups, not exclusive species. Knots
   may be unfamiliar. FLUTE is in the woodwind family despite metal construction.
8. BARK and ROOT have other meanings; CLOCK includes some other time devices in
   a broad sense. The category is intentionally broad and literal.
9. Pronunciation assumes common English; homophones can vary by dialect. Roman
   numerals are single letters by design, so tile length is not a clue to validity.
10. LIGHTHOUSE, HEADLINE, BEDROOM and KEYBOARD use closed compounds. Some tiles
    can form less-common compounds with another suffix; semantic alternate
    partitions have not been exhaustively evaluated.
